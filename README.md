<p align="justify"><i><span style="color:#DAA520">Beneath skies scorched by fire and ruin, he stood—armor charred, breath steady. Before him coiled the beast of legends: a dragon vast as a cathedral, eyes glowing with ancient malice. The air trembled with heat and history. Yet he did not flinch. His oath was older than fear.</span></i></p>

<p align="justify"><i><span style="color:#DAA520">The battle was not swift. Flame met steel, and roars shook the bones of forgotten kings. Each strike was a memory carved into his blade—of comrades lost, of villages razed, of promises made beside ashes. He did not fight for glory, but to silence the screaming that haunted his sleep.</span></i></p>

<p align="justify"><i><span style="color:#DAA520">The dragon fell with thunder in its throat. Its blood steamed across the stone, thick and dark like ink spilled over prophecy. The knight stood over the carcass, not in triumph, but mourning. What was slain was not just a monster, but a chapter—of myth, of meaning, of himself.</span></i></p>

<p align="justify"><i><span style="color:#DAA520">He sheathed his sword in silence. No songs would be sung for him. No statues carved. The people would rebuild, forgetting the name of the one who gave them dawn. But he asked for no memory. Only that peace be allowed to breathe where fire once ruled.</span></i></p>

<p align="justify"><i><span style="color:#DAA520">And so he walked on, into forests no map dared ink. Not as a hero, but as a man who once faced a dragon and lived—not just to tell the tale, but to carry its weight, alone, beneath the sky it nearly stole.</span></i></p>
